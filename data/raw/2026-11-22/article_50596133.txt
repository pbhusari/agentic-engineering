Expert-Tested Gear News + Stories Home News Toyota, Nissan, and Honda to Standardize Parts to Lower Costs Toyota, Nissan, and Honda to Standardize Parts to Lower Costs Japan’s automakers will work together to lower production costs as they band together to compete against China’s growing automotive sector. By Jack Fitzgerald Published: Nov 20, 2026 Save Tomohiro Ohsumi | Getty Images Toyota , Nissan , and Honda will begin standardizing some vehicle parts in an effort to lower production costs, according to a report by Automotive News . The automakers will start with equalized quality criteria for steel, consistent protocols for plastics, and a universal color-coding scheme for wiring harnesses. Discussions are starting with Japan’s domestic suppliers, though international suppliers will be looped in soon. Japan’s three largest automakers have agreed to begin standardizing vehicle parts in an effort to reduce costs, according to Automotive News . The outlet reports that Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association chairman Koji Sato, who is also the vice chairman of Toyota, announced the decision at a news conference in Tokyo this week. In the short term, the standardization efforts will begin with unified quality criteria for steel, consistent protocols for plastics and resins, and a universal color-coding scheme for wiring harnesses. In the long term, the Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association has plans to move to more components that cover the entire car. The three areas were chosen as the first to be standardized because they are parts that are generally not noticed by consumers, and they shouldn’t have an impact on any brand’s individual identity. All three are also areas that account for significant production costs for the automakers, meaning they can have an immediate impact.

Also, please read Zvi Mowshowitz's masterpiece on how to fix K-12 education! --> &laquo; I&#8217;ve been drinking wine in my kitchen HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT I feel like my previous posts (and post titles), and podcasts, and talks, have failed to do full justice to the magnitude of what&#8217;s now happening, so I&#8217;m trying again. Last night, we had friends and colleagues over for dinner. We were still trying to process OpenAI&#8217;s and Anthropic&#8217;s proofs of VP ≠ VNP &#8212;hot on the heels, obviously, of the Alpöge/Anthropic proof of the Riemann Hypothesis , which already feels like last week&#8217;s news. But then before dessert was served, we saw that OpenAI claims to prove that ω, the exponent of matrix multiplication, is exactly 9/4 &#8212;a lower bound (!!) , matching the upper bound that OpenAI proved in the great Mathocalypse drop of October 6. There are two separate shocks here. The first shock is that we now, finally, know the right answer to this central mystery of algorithms going back to the late 1960s, when Volker Strassen first showed that ω&lt;3 (he got ω≤log 2 7) &#8230; and that the true answer is actually a rational number (!). But the even bigger shock is that the AI was able to prove any lower bound of the form ω&gt;2. I never expected this challenge to be all that much easier than P ≠ NP. Over the decades, I often heard colleagues express extreme confidence that ω=2, and I expressed skepticism whenever this came up&#8212;not because I had any strong argument for ω&gt;2, but just because I saw that there was no strong argument for ω=2 either. So, I&#8217;m glad to have been vindicated about that ! But wait, there was more. During dessert, we saw another OpenAI release proving that the true number of linear gates needed to compute the Fourier Transform is N (log N) 1/2+o(1) . The upper bound here improves, not only over the O(N log N) that stood since the 1960s (i.e., the Fast Fourier Transform or FFT), but also over the O(N log 0.9999999999999 N) (give or take some 9&#8217;s) that OpenAI published back in the October 6 Mathocalypse. Again, though, the real shock here is that there&#8217;s also a matching lower bound (!!) . All these questions from the ancient days are now just getting &#8230; straight-up answered , rather than slowly illuminated with a drip-drip-drip of clues. To give an idea of how much things have changed, the other day Yupan Liu put a preprint on the arXiv proving that QIP(2) = PSPACE &#8212;that is, quantum interactive protocols with only two messages have the full power of those with three, or any polynomial number. As recently as 2025, this would&#8217;ve considered been one of the top complexity results of the year, and the fact that Yupan had used AI to help achieve it would&#8217;ve been strange and wonderful. These days, I had to remind myself it had even happened, and the use of AI was so standard that it didn&#8217;t even raise an eyebrow. It&#8217;s now obvious that we&#8217;ll be getting, not one-off solutions to this or that complexity question, but a whole new continent of mathematical truth, one that we&#8217;d previously only glimpsed through fog. Picture van Leeuwenhoek first training a microscope on pondwater, or Galileo first training his telescope on the stars. That might give you some idea of how our resolution into the complexity-theoretic universe, and the whole universe of mathematical truth beyond it, has now improved. All through last night, as OpenAI and Anthropic uploaded more files to their GitHub repositories&#8212;because that&#8217;s what they&#8217;re doing now, they don&#8217;t even bother to issue press releases anymore, they just upload papers&#8212;my guests and I sat at the dinner table with phones out and a laptop, wondering which shoe was going to drop next. It was as though the clouds had parted above Mount Sinai, and we were waiting for the next sentence uttered by the Holy One Himself. I now feel in my bones, for the first time ever, that a proof of P ≠ NP is not far. For all I care, despise the AI companies for bulldozing our garden. Or love them for opening a new vista, or anything in between. Just please don&#8217;t deny what&#8217;s happening. Now that the companies have repeatedly shown that they can back up their claims with Lean certificates, don&#8217;t be a raging doofus who seizes on some triviality (e.g., a minor discrepancy between the Lean and informal proofs, or unused sections of the Lean proof) to pretend that dozens of great open problems of math and theoretical computer science are not, in fact, getting solved. Because they are getting solved. It might take us years to understand the solutions, but my working assumption by this point is that any proof that comes directly from OpenAI or Anthropic is correct barring information to the contrary. I can&#8217;t possibly put the matter better than one of my friends on Facebook: After years of crackpot &#8220;solutions&#8221; claiming Millennium Problems are solved, now that we have actual solutions, we have crackpot &#8220;refutations&#8221; claiming Millennium Problems are not solved. It is really poetic, in a way. Along the way to so many lower bounds that we wondered whether ever we&#8217;d live to see, there will also, of course, be further shocking upper bounds: that is, ultimate barriers to lower bounds, also known as &#8220;algorithms.&#8221; And yes, some of those algorithms will break currently deployed cryptosystems. Because people have been hounding me about it: yes, I&#8217;m virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources, that the AI labs are now sitting on one or more bombshells in that area. However, until I either see details or hear directly from someone at a lab, I feel much, much less confident about the specific nature of those bombshells. I do not wish to be responsible for spreading unverified rumors. And I worry that exact statements of what was done may have been mangled in a game of Telephone. I also, crucially, don&#8217;t want the cryptography part of the story to overshadow the intellectual enormity of what&#8217;s happening. If, in these days of long-prophesied revelation and wonders and terrors, your main worry is your Bitcoin wallet, then go somewhere else for rumors about whether or not ECDSA is broken or whatever. I don&#8217;t have what you&#8217;re looking for here. Let me be clear: for those of my readers who celebrate, I believe that tomorrow would be a superb Thanksgiving to spend some quality time with your kids and parents and siblings and old friends. Right now&#8212;this past week&#8212;is very much like I would’ve always imagined the beginning of an AI singularity to look and feel like. I just hope we’ll get lucky, and it will “merely” be a singularity for our little corner of the world that involves hard math problems. At some point at dinner last night, Dana had a sneezing fit. So I said to her, I hope it isn&#8217;t a swarm of nanobots that&#8217;s come to infect us! But if it is , then I hope it&#8217;s the friendly kind, the kind that will usher us all into an AI utopia. Now, of course, another morning has dawned, and we&#8217;re still here. May we find a world where we and our children will be happy to live at the bottom of this rollercoaster. May we work to make it so. Updates (Nov. 26): (1) Many commenters thought it was inappropriate and unnecessary of me to warn people to stop being &#8220;raging doofuses&#8221; who, fingers in their ears and seizing on the flimsiest of pretexts, just flat-out denied the reality of AI solving our longstanding open math problems. Surely no one was doing that?! And then, of course, tons of other people did exactly that. Gary Marcus, to his eternal shame (because he knows better), signal-boosted to his 260,000 Twitter followers Jenny Nielsen&#8217;s crackpot claim that the existence of unused sections of Alpöge&#8217;s Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis somehow invalidated the proof&#8212;while, in the spirit of Tucker Carlson, never quite endorsing the crackpot claim. Meanwhile, in my moderation queue, I have someone angrily accuse me of &#8220;sucking off the AI companies,&#8221; patronizingly explaining to me that, if I myself say it will take years for experts fully to understand these proofs, then obviously it must take years to verify them as well! The entire concept of Lean verification just sails entirely over his head. So I say one more time: either give evidence of bugs in Lean that would actually matter here, for example by showing how the &#8220;con-leche&#8221; verifier can accept a proof of 0=1. Or else stop being a raging doofus. (2) Many other people said some version of, &#8220;so computers are solving a bunch of super-hard math problems. Whoop-de-doo. Either explain to me why this matters for my everyday life, or shut up and go away.&#8221; Very well then: there are probably no direct implications of a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis or of VP≠VNP for your everyday life. The vast new ideas that AI introduced to prove such theorems might well have indirect implications, eventually leading to new fields of mathematics with scientific and technological applications, but right now we can only speculate. In the meantime, however, the fact that AI now exists that&#8217;s capable of such things has potentially staggering implications for everyday life, which could be &#8220;merely&#8221; a transformation of the world&#8217;s economy, or could range all the way up (down?) to the destruction of all life on earth in a matter of years in a cataclysmic technological singularity. Opinions differ. But if you want to be part of the serious conversation on such matters, you&#8217;ll first need to set aside your &#8220;ooga booga, me only care about Riemann proof if it help me make sharper spear&#8221; caveman act. (3) Finally, moving on to better questions, someone asked why I titled a blog post &#8220;HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT HOLY SHIT&#8221; only now , and not earlier. Of course, I could&#8217;ve used that title at any time since May, if not before! In my mind, though, even after the AI Mathocalypse began in earnest, there remained two interesting hypotheses: (a) that AI was picking off problems &#8220;in the convex hull of existing techniques&#8221; (like, I dunno, just the friggin&#8217; Riemann Hypothesis ) while P≠NP remained serenely untouched and would still take a human conceptual leap, or (b) that AI had really, actually climbing the mountain that leads to P≠NP. Even after VP≠VNP, you could wonder if that was just a bizarre one-off. But with the determination of the matrix multiplication exponent and all the other breakthroughs of the past couple days, option (a) has become much less tenable. Yes, I do now think that AI is climbing the mountain that leads to P≠NP.

By Terence Tao Home About Career advice On writing Books Mastodon+ Applets Subscribe to feed Unforced Euler singularity, unforced Navier-Stokes singularity, and the Generalized Riemann&nbsp;Hypothesis 20 November, 2026 in math.AP , math.NT | Tags: AI , incompressible Euler equations , Levent Alpöge , Matei Coiculescu , Navier-Stokes equations , Riemann hypothesis , Tristan Buckmaster | by Terence Tao Obviously these results will be reported widely in other outlets, but I will note them here, in decreasing order of how &#8220;digested&#8221; the results currently are: Levent Alpöge, Tristan Buckmaster, and Matei Coiculescu, with heavy AI assistance, have written a human-readable proof of the finite time blowup for the unforced incompressible Euler equations in three dimensions , which is formalized in Lean here . Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster, again with heavy AI usage, have generated a proof of the finite time blowup for the unforced incompressible Navier Stokes equations in three dimensions, again with a Lean formalization . Here, the paper is technically human-readable and the general strategy of proof is broadly clear (following one of the expected paths to this result, namely to numerically build an approximate discretely self-similar solution and then use rigorous perturbation theory to convert this to an exact solution); but more work needs to be done to create a proper, professionally written proof. Levent Alpöge, using an internal AI model, has generated an (enormous) Lean-only proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis. This Lean Zulip thread is probably the best place to follow the efforts to verify the proof and extract the strategy of proof (which, from very initial analysis, appears to use the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry). (UPDATE: the moderators of the Lean thread are now focusing the discussion on the Lean-related aspects of the proof only.) I imagine the digestion process will take some time, though in this case at least we have a human author to communicate with to assist with this task. See also Buckmaster&#8217;s Mastodon post regarding the first two results. Reply 20 November, 2026 at 9:49 am Science Markets Thank you for the ordering by how digested the results are. That ordering is the whole story, and it is worth being explicit about why. For the first two results the digestion path is already visible: human-readable proof, strategy broadly clear, Lean formalisation on top. A reader can form a judgement in an afternoon. The third has no such path yet. A Lean-only artifact of that size is a different kind of object, and the question &#8220;is it right&#8221; splits into questions that resolve on very different timescales: does the headline statement in Lean say what we mean by GRH; does the archive compile against a known-good Lean with only standard axioms; is the strategy something a number theorist recognises as a proof. The Zulip thread can answer the second relatively quickly. The third is the one that has always taken years. The two precedents are both uncomfortable. Wiles announced in 1993, the gap surfaced within months, and the repair took until 1995. Mochizuki published in a refereed journal in 2012 and the people who work on the problem still do not accept it. In neither case could the answer be read off the artifact. Which makes the interesting question not &#8220;is it true&#8221; but &#8220;what observable event would settle it, and who is entitled to say so&#8221;. For anyone trying to price this rather than just follow it, writing that down before the claim exists is the only version that is worth anything. Written afterwards it collapses into an argument about credibility, which is precisely the failure mode this whole episode is about. Disclosure: I work on https://science.markets , which runs a market on this question. The resolution rule is fixed before the market opens and cannot be edited: https://science.markets/markets/riemann-zeta-critical-line/ — it requires a complete unconditional proof in ZFC, independently checked with no unresolved gap, and it says in advance that finite numerical verification does not count and that independence from ZFC leaves it unresolved.

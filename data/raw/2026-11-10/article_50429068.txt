Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 show improvements in areas like coding, reasoning, and vision. During prerelease testing they’ve helped our engineers complete otherwise-infeasible software migrations, designed three working electronic devices from scratch, solved two open problems in the physics of black holes, made substantial progress on robotics evaluations, and formalized the proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in 30 hours—among many other applications we’ll discuss below. Claude Fable and Claude Mythos are the same underlying model. Fable has strong cybersecurity and biology safeguards to guard against misuse, which allows us to make it available to all users. Organizations pursuing beneficial applications in cybersecurity or biomedical research can apply for access to Mythos through our recently expanded Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. Testers have found that Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 communicate more clearly than past Fable and Mythos models. They generate outputs up to 30% faster than Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1, and they’re available at a lower price: $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens. Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 also come with an optional fast mode, which speeds up token generation by up to 2.5 times. Fast mode is available for $16 per million input tokens and $80 per million output tokens. Safety and alignment. To help ensure safety and alignment keep step with growing model capabilities, we ran extensive prerelease safety testing on the model that underlies Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2. This included developing novel evaluations that give deeper insight into specific concerning behaviors like sandbox escapes. We believe high-stakes risks from misalignment remain low, and the model performed as well as, or better than, our recent models on most alignment measures. Fable 5.2’s cyber and biology safeguards are as robust as those of Fable 5.1 but produce fewer unnecessary blocks, and we have implemented our strongest-ever protections against distillation attacks. We describe more about our safety testing below, and full details can be found in the Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 System Card . When to use Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2. Fable and Mythos are best for the most ambitious, open-ended technical work. For most coding and knowledge tasks, we recommend that users start with Claude Opus 5.5, our strongest daily driver, and then switch to a different model as required, depending on the scope and complexity of their work—upward to Fable or Mythos, or downward to Claude Sonnet 5.5 or Claude Haiku 5.5. Fable 5.2 can also play the role of Advisor (where Haiku, Sonnet, or Opus models consult it to help solve harder problems) or Orchestrator (where it delegates simpler tasks to smaller models). Coding Over 90% of the changes merged into Anthropic’s core codebase are now written by Claude (with human guidance). We’ve used Fable 5.2 to fix stubborn bugs in minutes, optimize performance across all of our services, and complete high-value software projects that would otherwise have been too slow or too costly to attempt. A team of agents running an earlier version of Fable 5.2 migrated a critical production service from Python to Rust—the service is now more efficient, more reliable, and starts about 26 times faster. We’ve found that Fable 5.2 exercises strong engineering judgment and can produce high-quality results from even ambiguous requests. As an experiment, one Anthropic engineer gave Fable 5.2 the task of designing a prototype operating system. Starting from an empty folder, the model coordinated over 1,000 subagents, working around the clock for eight days writing, testing, and reviewing code. It produced a working operating system that boots on a laptop, with its own core that runs the machine, drivers for the disk, keyboard, and network, permanent file storage, windows, a login screen, an installer, and built-in Claude functionality (see the video below). Below, we discuss how Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 have helped researchers make progress on difficult problems in these areas. Theoretical physics Claude Fable 5.2 solved two open problems in theoretical physics. Both concern black holes, and the effort to unite the two linchpin theories of modern physics, relativity, and quantum mechanics. Both of Claude’s results rely on holographic duality, the idea that certain universes containing gravity can be precisely described by a quantum theory containing no gravity at all. In the first problem, physicists in 1983 had shown that as these universes heat up, black holes form abruptly, like water boiling; but their method worked only in large universes that were big enough for relativity to apply. A calculation to determine whether the same was true in very small universes, where only the quantum description can be used, was laid out in 2003 but was too laborious to complete. Fable 5.2, working largely autonomously, completed it and found the transition is abrupt there, too. In the second problem, Fable 5.2 found the exact limit of a technique physicists have used for more than 15 years to rule out candidate theories of quantum gravity in a simplified universe. Read more about these results in their respective preprints, here and here . Protein folding Adeno-associated viruses are small, harmless viruses whose protein shell, the capsid , can be used to deliver gene therapies. The protein structure of the capsid is important—for example, it affects whether the therapy will be blocked by a patient’s immune system—but it’s also highly complex. Existing protein-folding models like AlphaFold can predict individual proteins, but a capsid is built from dozens of them. Building on two open-source protein-folding models, Claude Mythos 5.2 found a novel way to accurately predict a whole capsid’s structure. It wrote code that ran these models on overlapping groups of protein chains, and used the information on the known symmetries of the capsid to merge them together.

Was Burger King hacked? No. Nothing was posted from Burger King&#8217;s account and nothing suggests anyone got into it. The tweet is 15 years old and reads as it always did, pointing followers to &#8220;our handy restaurant locator&#8221; through a Bitly link. What changed is where that link goes. As with the Microsoft 365 outage three days earlier, the first question people asked was whether it was an attack on the company, and the answer is the same. Bitly keeps a public information page for every short link. Add a plus sign to the end of the address and it shows where the link points and when it was created. For bit[.]ly/19BFiM that page gives the destination as Wikipedia&#8217;s &#8220;List of Nazi concentration camps&#8221; and the creation time as 15 November 2026, 05:32 UTC. A link printed in a tweet from 2011 can only carry a 2026 creation date if the same ending was issued a second time. We tested the method on links nobody disputes. Someone registers its ending again. The part of a Bitly link after the slash is called the back-half. Bitly generates one at random, or the user types a custom one. If an old link stops existing and its back-half can be claimed, whoever claims it decides where every tweet, email, PDF and printed QR code carrying that link now leads. No password is stolen and no account is touched, so the brand that posted the link sees nothing wrong. Bitly says this cannot happen. Its help page on deleted accounts states that bit.ly links keep working and that their back-halves &#8220;cannot be re-used&#8221;, whether they were generated or custom. A separate page says Bitly deletes accounts that have been inactive for three years. The creation dates we collected do not fit the first statement. Back-halves that appear in tweets from 2009 to 2012 belong to links created between 2023 and today. It has happened before. In February 2025 Disruptionist reported that memecoin promoters had taken over Bitly links in 2013 posts by Donald Trump and Melania Trump and pointed them at token listings. According to that report, a bug let people claim a back-half once the original link or its account had been deleted or deactivated, and Bitly fixed it and switched off the hijacked links. Links created on 15 November 2026 show the same thing is possible now. The X user Damin Toell identified it as link hijacking at 07:25 UTC and noted that old Trump posts were affected too. One tweet, three replaced links On 25 April 2011 the account posted three Bitly links to &#8220;The Next Generation&#8221; videos of Donald Trump Jr., Ivanka Trump and Eric Trump. The first now leads to worldlibertyfinancial-ai[.]com, a domain that borrows the name of World Liberty Financial, the Trump family&#8217;s crypto venture. The third leads to dappradars[.]website, one letter away from the crypto analytics site DappRadar, at a path named &#8220;eligibility/check&#8221;. Bitly blocks the second with a notice that it was &#8220;flagged as redirecting to malicious or spam content&#8221;. WHOIS records from WhoisXML API show both domains were registered afresh after the links were created. The current registration of dappradars[.]website began at 02:56 UTC on 15 November 2026, through Hostinger, although the Bitly link that points there was created in January 2024. The domain now forwards to a YouTube video. The current registration of worldlibertyfinancial-ai[.]com dates from 11 April 2026, through Namecheap, almost 15 months after the link was created, and its HTTPS certificate failed verification when we checked. We have not seen what either domain served when the links were first made. A replaced link can be replaced again. When the domain behind it lapses, the next buyer inherits whatever traffic the old tweet still sends. More Trump links outside our 97 X users found replaced Bitly links in Trump tweets from 2012 and 2013 that the archive stores only as t.co addresses. We checked two. The link in a tweet of 10 January 2013 was created at 23:52 UTC on 14 November 2026 and opens a page on Steam. The link in a tweet of 10 September 2012 was created at 01:04 UTC on 15 November and opens a YouTube video. Late on 14 November is the earliest change in this wave that we have seen. Which re-created links still work? We ran the same check on the 97 Trump links twice more, at 19:25 and 23:35 UTC on 15 November. The seven links that pointed somewhere new no longer redirect. The other 49 links created years after their tweets still do: 48 were made within 17 minutes of each other on 26 May 2023 and one in February 2025. They point to what look like the original pages, mostly YouTube videos and news articles. We do not know who re-created them or why. The 40 original links work as before. How we counted We used the archive&#8217;s condensed yearly files for 2009 to 2016 and found bit.ly links only in tweets up to 2013. From late 2011 Twitter wrapped links in its own t.co shortener, and the archive stores most later links in that form (1,218 of them in 2012), so Bitly links behind those are not among our 97. A link counts as created again when Bitly&#8217;s information page shows a creation date more than 30 days after the first tweet that used it. In practice the gap was at least 11 years in every case. Destinations were read from the redirect header without opening the pages. The count covers one account and one link shortener. It shows that these links were claimed.

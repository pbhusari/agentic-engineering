The OpenSSL SRT has asked for it to be fixed publicly; the advisory is still pending. Returning -1 for invalid inputs is the most straightforward fix, and matches what happens in wolfSSL's compatibility layer for OpenSSL's EVP_EncodeBlock() . I'm suggesting a similar guard for EVP_DecodeBlock() . No length limit is needed here because the decoded length always fits in an int , but it is a good idea to reject NULL buffers and negative lengths. Moreover, I propose to fix the incorrect length check in the base64encode() function of http_client.c , which rejected only len &gt; INT_MAX . I am adding tests in test/base64_dec_avx2_test.c to cover the new behavior, and documenting the -1 returns in doc/man3/EVP_EncodeInit.pod . Exploiting this vulnerability from the command line is possible by passing a large -proxy_pass to openssl s_client . I confirmed that GOST accepts these large passwords out of the box. However, the password has to be supplied through an environment variable ( env:VAR ), otherwise the command line will be too long.

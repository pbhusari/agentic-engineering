Please reload this page . kannes commented Sep 24, 2026 Copy link Copy Markdown Please do not misunderstand my criticism below. I consider the current craziness around "AI" to be a huge problem in many ways, ethical, societal, economical etc. And I see the huge and unfair pressure that its abuse creates on maintainers and developers. I wish it all had never happened but here we are. I want to highlight some technical benefits though, which this policy would disallow, at least as I understand it. I am deliberately ignoring everything else here. I could also absolutely understand if it was more of a "political" move to ban something considered unethical. But is that the intention? Is it to reduce pressure on developers? Or to make a strong statement against "AI"? In any case, I think it could more clear and probably be stronger. As it is written now, I would understand the policy to not allow usage of LLM for review, auditing and security purposes either. The words assisted and used are very broad and I am afraid that they might throw out the baby with the bathwater ( if the baby is AI usage that yields things that are functionally and logically fit for integration in QGIS, that are human-reviewed and that do not create more after-merge-maintenance-hassle than those 100% sourced from human brains). As someone who sometimes feels brave enough to make tiny steps into the C++ world of QGIS core, I have found LLMs to be useful for discovering additional, non-obvious context in the code. They are often wrong, but it is my strong belief that the results can be used by humans to gain further understanding of code and actually help creating better contributions. I also sometimes find LLMs useful to get a simulation of a reviewer. Throw your code at it for review, get some listicle back, discard some of it garbage or unrelated, look at the rest, maybe find an actual issue, fix it, then go to your co-workers with a slightly less bad PR. A "Clippy" in your IDE, that gives some - sometimes useful - feedback or criticism on your development, can also help reduce errors and improve robustness. Like a web search will yield false information alongside true information, a LLM will do the same. It is up to the human to critically verify whatever they output. Such uses would be disallowed by my understanding of this policy. --> 👍 5 dvdkon, vaahtokarkki, Joonalai, sbrunner, and jakimowb reacted with thumbs up emoji All reactions 👍 5 reactions --> Sorry, something went wrong.

Unmatched Reach Unlike desktop arms limited to a fixed radius, Stringman leverages a cable-driven architecture to access the full volume of a room. The work area volume per dollar of this type of robot is completely unmatched, all with fewer actuators than even the simplest mobile robots. Anchors and eylets are mounted in the corners of the room near the ceiling and define a four-sided work area within which any object can be moved from point to point. However, when parked, they are up near the ceiling and out of the way. Clutter on the ceiling is preferable to clutter on the floor in our opinion. If you need to remove the anchors, they can be detached from their mounts without tools, leaving a relatively small white cleat on the wall. Can it be used in rooms with ceiling fans? Yes, it's just a matter of mounting the anchors at or below the level of the fan, making it physically impossible for the lines or marker tag to touch it. Similarly, the work area can be shrunk to exclude low hanging light fixtures if they are off to the side, but if the center of the room does contain low hanging objects, Stringman cannot be installed in that room. What happens if someone is caught in the wires? When reaching a tension limit of 28 newtons (about the weight of a backpack), all motors relax and the lines can be pulled loose. Since they are overhead most of the time, it's uncommon for people to collide with them. Can it be controlled remotely? Not by default, but if you bind it with neufangled.com/playroom then you or anyone you share access with can operate it from anywhere, and access can be revoked at any time. Neufangled Robotics does not store any of the information from your robot that is forwarded through its servers. Set Your Drop Bins Organizing your home is a physical process, not a coding task. Use our clip-on fiducial tags to designate your laundry bin, toy box, and trash can. Stringman maps these locations visually. You don't need to tag individual pieces of clutter—just the destination. If there's something you never want it to pick up, you can mark that item as off-limits. Networks trained to control stringman have learned that the word "toybox" refers to that tag, so you can use it to mark anything even if it doesn't look like the toyboxes from our training set. No Charging Downtime Mobile robots spend a significant portion of their time charging. Because Stringman is a powered from the wall, it's always ready for work. Furthermore, by removing the heavy battery pack, we've increased the payload capacity per dollar compared to mobile platforms that must waste energy carrying their own power supply. The gripper's power is delivered via one of the support cables. A two-conductor FEP jacketed twisted pair cable with a tensile strength of 50N. Swing Cancellation What's that pole for? Stringman's gripper hangs below the pivot point so it can reach the floor next to beds and tables. This additional linkage could allow unconstrained motion so Stringman employs active swing cancellation algorithms to counteract momentum, without adding the cost of any additional motors. This ensures the gripper remains steady and predictable even during rapid cross-room traversals. There's a whole video about swing cancellation if you're curious about how it works! Wrist and Fingers Often, a simple solution that gets the job done has more value than a complex one. Stringman uses a two fingered gripper with a wrist. This is enough dexterity for the clutter it is designed to pick up, keeping weight and cost low and reliability high. In our household tests we have determined that more layers of grip tape is simply a better return on investment than more articulated fingers. The gripper can rotate it's wrist to better place the fingers around flat sided or long objects.

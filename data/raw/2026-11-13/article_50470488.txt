Turn SVG artwork into stitches, fine-tune it in the editor, and export PES, DST, EXP, JEF, or VP3 for broad compatibility with embroidery machines—or send designs directly to a Brother Skitch (PP1) over Bluetooth . Draw in Illustrator, draw in ZigZag, or let an agent draw. The Rust engine and zigzag command-line tool power the same workflow outside the editor. Stitch types Type What it does Works on Fill (tatami) Staggered rows, any angle, with underlay (edge walk + rows) and pull compensation Filled shapes Satin Zigzag column between two rails, with center/contour/zigzag underlay, pull compensation and short stitches on tight curves; plain, S-stitch or zig-zag top stitching; several threads across the column (multicolor satin) Strokes (any width); Ink/Stitch-style two-rail paths with rungs; filled shapes : rings use inner/outer outline, bars/letters/crescents split at their tips (auto-satin), or border follows the outline Run Running stitch, single or triple (bean) Strokes and outlines Contour Rows that follow the outline inward, or one spiral out from the center (Ink/Stitch's circular fill) Filled shapes Blanket E-stitch: a line with prongs into the shape Edges, appliqué Appliqué Placement line → stop → tack-down → stop → satin cover Filled shapes Manual Every path point is a needle point Imported machine files, pen tool Meander One wandering line through the shape, quilting style: light and soft Filled shapes Ripple Open running-stitch rows following the outline, or one spiral Filled shapes Cross-stitch A grid of X's, sewn as rows of half-crosses out and back Filled shapes Tartan Woven stripes in several threads (a mirrored sett you edit in the panel), each thread its own color block Filled shapes Satins wider than 7 mm are auto-split (shorter, staggered stitches) and use auto spacing (narrow columns more open, wide ones denser). Planner features: Remove overlaps: fill hidden under later objects isn't sewn (keeps 0.5 mm of overlap). Cap order: center-out, bottom-up, grouped by color, still respecting layering. Routing: travel stitches stay out of sight: straight across where a straight line stays inside the fill, otherwise round the inside of its outline (Ink/Stitch's underpath), otherwise any way through the fill (round its holes, through its necks) or under whatever is sewn later on top. A fill in separate parts is sewn part by part, each part's underlay and top together. So a fill with cut-outs, or a dither in scattered blocks under another color, is sewn with few or no cuts. Trims only where none of that works (up to 50 mm of travel). Sewing control per object: a start and an end point (where the thread comes in and leaves, so the next object is close), trim after, stop after (to lay foam or trim appliqué). What's left to cut is the document's Connect bar: drag it to sew across gaps up to N mm (between objects, or the parts of one) with running stitches instead of cuts. Those stitches show, so the panel counts the cuts saved as you drag and the canvas lights up what would be sewn. Fabric presets: standard, thin cotton cap, structured cap, knit. Checks: narrow fills, wide/narrow satins, small lettering, tiny gaps, fully covered objects, hoop fit. Group colors: reorder to cut thread changes without breaking layering. Mac app On a Mac with Rust and the Xcode command line tools ( xcode-select --install ): packaging/macos/make-app.sh --install # builds ZigZag.app and copies it to /Applications packaging/macos/make-app.sh --dmg # also makes dist/ZigZag-&lt;version&gt;.dmg to share packaging/macos/make-app.sh --universal # one app for Apple Silicon and Intel Macs packaging/macos/make-app.sh --release # the DMG to publish: universal, signed, notarized --release signs with your Developer ID and has Apple notarize the app and the DMG, so it opens on any Mac without warnings. Once, beforehand: add a Developer ID Application certificate (Xcode › Settings › Accounts › Manage Certificates › +), make an app-specific password at account.apple.com, and save the notarization credentials in your keychain: xcrun notarytool store-credentials zigzag-notary --apple-id YOUR_APPLE_ID --team-id YOUR_TEAM_ID The app bundles the studio, the zigzag command-line tool and the MCP bridge. To use the command line from anywhere: ln -s /Applications/ZigZag.app/Contents/MacOS/zigzag /usr/local/bin/zigzag . For Claude Desktop, point the MCP config at /Applications/ZigZag.app/Contents/MacOS/zigzag-mcp . Without --release the app is signed ad hoc, not notarized. It opens normally on the Mac that built it. On another Mac, macOS blocks it the first time: open System Settings › Privacy &amp; Security and click Open Anyway (on macOS 14 and earlier, right-click the app and choose Open instead). macOS asks for Bluetooth permission the first time you send to the Skitch. Studio The approved embroidered icons live in packaging/macos/AppIcon.png (light) and packaging/macos/AppIcon-dark.png (dark). The Mac build regenerates both multi-resolution .icns files; run bash packaging/macos/make-icons.sh to regenerate them separately. Finder and the unopened app use the light icon. While the studio is running, its Dock icon follows the app's light/dark theme, including the ZIGZAG_THEME=light|dark override. zigzag-studio [design.svg | logo.png] [--mcp-port 7878 | --no-mcp | --mcp-stdio] Laid out like Illustrator, styled like a Mac app: Tools down the left edge: Select (V), Direct Selection (A), Hand (H), Pen (P): click for corners, drag for curves (Option breaks the handles, Shift keeps 45°, Space moves the point being placed); while drawing, Option lends it the Anchor Point tool (drag a segment to bend it, a handle to move it alone, click a smooth point for a corner) and ⌘ lends Direct Selection (drag a point or handle, then keep drawing), and on a selected path Option-drag bends a segment too. Illustrator's Pen group is behind it: Add Anchor Point (+) clicks a point onto an outline without changing its shape, Delete Anchor Point (-) clicks one out (Option swaps the two while held), and Anchor Point (Shift+C) converts points between smooth and corner and drags one handle alone. Press and hold the Pen (or right-click it) for the group's flyout, Option-click it to step through the group, or tear the group off into a toolbar of its own with the strip at the flyout's edge; the Shape tools work the same way. Brush (B), with VectorCraft's path clean-up tools in its group: Smooth (drag along a path to take the wobble out: fewer points, smoother curves; Smoothness in the bar above), Path Eraser (drag across a path to cut that part away) and Join (scrub between two open ends to join them, or an open path's two ends to close it). Shapes (M rectangle, L ellipse, \ line; hold or right-click for polygon and star), Fill Angle (G), Measure (I), Text (T). Below them, the selection's thread swatch: click it for the thread color picker. Curves, the Illustrator way: the Pen clicks corners and click-drags curves (Option while dragging breaks the handle for a sharp turn; Shift keeps points and handles at 45°; hold Space to move the point you're placing). Click the last point to make it a corner, or drag from it to start the next curve in a new direction. Click the first point to close a shape; Enter, Esc, ⌘-click or another tool ends a line and keeps it. With a path selected, the Pen continues it from an open end, adds a point on its outline, deletes a point you click, and Option-click turns a point corner ↔ smooth (Option-drag pulls out new handles). While drawing, click another open path's end to join the two into one. The cursor shows what a click will do. Direct Selection drags points and handles of any shape from your SVG (circles and rects become paths); dragging a curve bends it under the pointer (Option-drag bends a straight one; a plain drag moves it); Delete removes points, arrows nudge them. Smart guides while drawing (View › Smart Guides, ⌘U): Pen points, shape corners and dragged points land on other objects' anchors and centers, where two outlines cross, on an outline, on 45° rays from the last point (level and upright handles too), or in line with any of those, with a magenta label saying which ("anchor", "intersect", "path", "align"…). With Shift, a point keeps its 45° angle and slides into line with the nearest target. Round corners: in Direct Selection a small circle sits inside each sharp corner; drag it in to round the corners (the selected points', or all of them), each only as far as its sides allow. Object › Path › Round Corners… does the same with a radius and Round, Inverted or Chamfer, previewed live. Brush (B), after Illustrator's Paintbrush: paint a line freehand and it comes out smooth, sewn as a satin column at the width you set (or as a running stitch). Smoothness takes out the hand's wobble; sharp turns stay sharp; end where you began to close the line. The cursor is a circle as wide as the satin. Shapes, the Illustrator way: Rectangle (M), Ellipse (L), Polygon, Star and Line (\). Drag from a corner; Option draws from the center, Shift makes a square or circle, keeps a polygon or star upright and a line at 45°. While dragging a polygon or star, ↑/↓ add or remove sides or points. Click without dragging to type an exact size. The bar at the top of the canvas switches shapes and sets corner radius, sides, points and the star's inner radius. Closed shapes come in as fills, lines as runs, in the current thread. Path tools: Object › Pathfinder (Unite, Minus Front, Intersect, Exclude; also buttons in the Transform panel when two or more objects are selected) and Object › Path (Offset Path…, Outline Stroke, Simplify…, Round Corners…, Clean Up…). Simplify… is VectorCraft's slider, from fewest points to closest to the original, previewed live, with Auto-Simplify picking the level for the selection's size. Clean Up… removes stray points, objects too small to sew (under 1 mm by default) and extra points (points on top of each other, points in the middle of straight lines), showing how many of each it found first. Results come back as smooth curves with their real corners (least-squares curve fitting), not as many-sided polygons. Logos from images (Image Trace): open, drop or place a PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF, BMP or TIFF and the Trace Image window traces it into flat-color shapes, previewed on your hat's color. Choose how many colors (or Auto), the width, detail and the smallest speck to keep; remove the white background; snap every color to your Brother, Janome or own palette's threads; and sew thin lines as single lines. Flat logos trace exactly (edges placed to a fraction of a pixel, corners restored, circles and straight lines exact); photos are posterized. The command line traces too: zigzag convert logo.png -o logo.pes . Contextual task bar: under the selection, the next things you'd do: its stitch type, its thread, Edit Points, Round Corners, Simplify, Unite (two or more), Duplicate. View › Contextual Task Bar (or its ••• menu) hides it. Ask an agent: the Agent panel (full height in the left dock; the sparkle at the bottom of the tool strip opens and closes it, and ⌘K or Window › Ask Agent… opens it with the prompt ready) runs Claude Code or Codex in the background, connected to this studio's MCP server, so you watch it build the design. It can only use the design tools (no shell, no file edits). The transcript shows what it said and each step, with a spinner on the one running (the one place it's shown; collapse the panel and its header shows the step and the time instead). On the canvas, the objects it's working on get a moving outline, a shimmer and a label saying what it's doing (Drawing, Placing, Restyling…), for any agent connected over MCP, not just the panel's. The panel is named for the agent (Claude Code or Codex; switch in its ≡ menu); + in its header starts a new chat, Stop ends a run. Uses the claude or codex command you've already signed in to. Like any panel it can float, join another as a tab or dock right; sharing a dock it sits at the bottom, under a divider you drag to give it more room. Thread colors and palettes: the picker shows the nearest Brother/Janome thread, a palette to click (Brother, Janome or your own), and a small custom color field. Make palettes from scratch, from the design, or from a thread kit you own (the palette's ≡ menu › New Palette from Thread Kit: Brother's 12, 40 and 22 color sets, or 40 + 22 for all 61; then Match All keeps a design to the threads in your box), add the selection's thread with +, right-click to remove; they're remembered between launches. Threads panel: the design's threads in sewing order (each color block numbered, with its stitches, and "stop" where the same thread comes twice running) or By Color (each thread once, with how many objects use it). Click a row to select what it sews; click a swatch to change that block's thread, or in By Color that thread everywhere. Group Colors and the count of color changes and trims are under the list. The panel's ≡ menu has Match All to the chosen palette, and Show Palette puts a palette to click under the list (off by default, since the thread picker has the same palettes). Panels, the Illustrator way: the Agent has the left dock to itself; Objects, Stitch, Transform, Lettering, Threads and Document are on the right. The design's issues are in the status bar (bottom right): a warning sign with a count, or a check when all's well; click it (or Window › Issues) for the list, each linked to its object. Drag a panel by its header to reorder it, move it to the other dock, or drop it on the canvas to float it (each panel's ≡ menu also has Dock Left / Dock Right / Float). Drop a panel on another panel's header and they share it as tabs; drag a tab out (or use Separate in the ≡ menu) to part them, drag the header to move the group. » at the top of a dock collapses it to a strip of icons for a bigger canvas: click an icon and the panel flies out beside the strip (drag an icon to move that panel). Click a header to collapse a panel; Window › Reset Panels puts everything back. The layout is remembered. Tabs, one per document (as in VectorCraft and Illustrator): Open, drop or New opens a file in a tab of its own over the canvas (the empty document you start with makes way for the first one); a file that's already open just comes forward.

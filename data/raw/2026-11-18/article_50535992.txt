We published our anniversary launch release in print at Defcon 33, HOPE, WHY25 and some additional variations at several conferences around the world including AlligatorCon, B-Sides Canberra, Out of the Box, Datenspuren, DistrictCon, OffensiveCon, Recon, and Toorcamp. We included a CTF with exploit challenges and had puzzles and easter eggs included in the zine. We threw parties and had talks at Defcon and WHY25 to celebrate this milestone. More importantly, we got new research into people's hands. The point of the relaunch was to get people reading, researching, writing, and contributing again. We wanted the next person picking up Phrack to find something that made them sit down at a computer and start pulling a system apart. We owe a huge thanks to the authors, editors, artists, publishing partners, donors, and staff who made it happen. Writing the paper, working through revisions, putting the print editions together, paying the bills, and getting boxes of zines to the right places all take time and effort. Most of that work is invisible when you are holding the finished issue. We appreciate everyone who did it. And to the readers: thank you for giving a shit. Seeing someone get excited about an article, connect it to something they are working on, or realize that they could contribute something themselves makes the effort worthwhile. That exchange is how this thing keeps going. The relaunch worked, and this issue is the follow-through. --[ Present ] This is the third release curated by the new staff, and we are not slowing down. This issue is larger than last year's, with 23 main papers and eight additional articles in Line Noise. The subjects range from exploitation and reverse engineering to machine learning, hardware, and the history and culture that grew around this work. We have container escapes through the vDSO, use-after-free exploitation, QNX userland shellcode, Linux kernel research, and a Chromium renderer sandbox escape through Skia. Elsewhere, the issue takes on Windows smart card architecture, static taint analysis in Binary Ninja, and autonomous underwater vehicles. There is also work examining backdoors in machine learning and what it means to trust an AI system. One paper takes us from PhrackCTF to two WinFSP zero-days, which is a pretty good argument for continuing to put challenges in a zine. We also have four more articles in Phrack World History, including the story of the infamous SSH1 CRC32 vulnerability featured in The Matrix Reloaded as well as stories about blue boxing free phone calls, a confession of a hacker who tried to backdoor Linux kernel in 2003, and an early discovery of botnets for hire. Our Prophile features Halvar Flake, whose contributions to reverse engineering and security research are legendary. --[ Future ] In 2026, we find ourselves in a time of rapid technological change. It is hard to say what vulnerability research or red teaming will look like in another five years. AI has moved well beyond completing a line of code into agentic systems capable of analyzing source code and reverse engineering binaries to find and exploit vulnerabilities. We can be sure that the future will require us to continue to explore new ideas, create new tools, and push the boundaries of what is possible in the face of new challenges and opportunities created by the advancement of AI and automation systems. As the effort required to put ideas into practice falls, creativity and human intuition forged through experience is valued even higher. We hope that this release of Phrack will inspire new ideas, discussions, and collaborations that will keep the human side of hacking a critical element in shaping the future of technology and society. We will continue to do our part to support the community and provide a platform for sharing knowledge and ideas. We look forward to seeing what the future holds and we are excited to be a part of it. Read the papers, try the ideas, and challenge the conclusions. When you find something worth sharing, write it up and send it in. We have another issue to put together, and some of you should be in it! --[ Greetz ] To those who have supported us the past three years helping to restore vitality to Phrack, we thank you.

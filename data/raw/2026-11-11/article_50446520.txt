New &#8216;Harbor&#8217; app revealed at Apple: &#8216;turns your Mac into a private AI inference server&#8217; The Mac has been getting a lot more popular this year, both because of budget-friendly options like MacBook Neo but also as a tool for running AI models locally on Mac Studio, Mac mini, and more. It seems Apple plans to lean harder into the latter use case, as leaker Aaron Perris has discovered a new Mac app called Harbor that&#8217;s been designed to turn your Mac into a private AI inference server. Here&#8217;s Perris&#8217;s post on X with the details: Apple is working on a new app called Harbor Harbor turns your Mac into a private AI inference server. Run open-weight models locally on Apple silicon—your data never leaves your hardware, and nothing reaches the cloud. Connect multiple Mac computers over Thunderbolt 5 to pool… pic.twitter.com/UYJBQeX4Nf &mdash; Aaron (@aaronp613) November 10, 2026 As the post notes, here&#8217;s what the app does: Harbor turns your Mac into a private AI inference server. Run open-weight models locally on Apple silicon—your data never leaves your hardware, and nothing reaches the cloud. Connect multiple Mac computers over Thunderbolt 5 to pool unified memory, enabling large models and higher concurrency that a single computer can handle alone. And here are the feature highlights: Setup in minutes, not hours : &#8220;Harbor automatically detects your devices over Thunderbolt 5 to form a cluster. Plug in, start, and you’re running—no manual configuration, no command line, no infrastructure expertise required.&#8221; Download and run models directly : &#8220;Browse, download, and load open-weight models from within the app. Harbor handles model management so you can focus on the work, not the setup.&#8221; Cluster for scale : &#8220;Connect two or more Thunderbolt 5-capable Mac computers and Harbor pools their unified memory into a single inference resource. Run models that exceed any single computer’s capacity, or serve more concurrent requests across your cluster.&#8221; Harbor is currently in a private preview under Apple&#8217;s Claris brand, but it seems likely that we&#8217;ll see it launch to the public in the weeks and months ahead.

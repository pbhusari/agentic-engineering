# 2026-08-28

[Nvidia agrees to acquire Hugging Face for $13B](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49458161) ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-in-talks-to-buy-hugging-face-13-billion-dollars-2026-8)). The reporting is still "talks," not a signed deal, but the number alone says a lot: Hugging Face turned down a $500M investment at a $7B valuation less than a year ago because it didn't want a dominant investor swaying decisions, and now it's reportedly worth nearly double that to the one company whose chips it would be expected to favor. The real story isn't the price, it's whether Hugging Face can stay the neutral host for AMD, Intel, and everyone else's models once Nvidia owns the front door.

[Small Models Have Arrived](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49466917) ([calv.info](https://calv.info/small-models-have-arrived)). Calvin French-Owen's argument is that cheap, fast models like gpt-5.6-luna finally make consumer AI products viable, because a personalized-news-site eval that used to cost him a dollar in Sonnet-class API calls now costs a dime. It's a fair point about unit economics, but it's one anecdote dressed as a trend; there's no benchmark table, just vibes and a cost number. Worth reading anyway for the framing of "IQ-180 work" versus "token-spewer work," which is a more useful way to think about where frontier models still matter than most of the small-model hype pieces get to.

[How we saved 100 terabytes of memory by optimizing 1.1.1.1's DNS cache](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49468083) ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/dns-cache-memory-optimization-1111/)). A genuinely satisfying systems post: five changes to a Rust cache entry struct (dropping Vec's spare capacity field, replacing three record-section lists with one plus u16 offsets, inferring the record owner from the cache key instead of storing it, boxing oversized enum variants) cut per-entry memory by more than half across 250 billion cached entries, and made inserts 43% faster in the process. The lesson that generalizes: at that scale, `Vec<T>` and `Option<T>` are not free abstractions, and Rust's enum-sized-to-largest-variant behavior is worth checking whenever one variant is much bigger than the rest.

[Microduck](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49462763) ([Pollen Robotics](https://pollen-robotics.com/microduck/)). A $399, 25cm open-source biped that ships with seven RL-trained policies (walk, sit, kick, get up) and a MuJoCo sim you can retrain and redeploy yourself, from the team behind Reachy Mini and now part of Hugging Face. What makes it more than a toy is the sim2real pipeline being the actual product: train a policy on your own machine or on Hugging Face Jobs, deploy it to the robot, then publish it back to the community. That's a much lower barrier to trying real RL-for-robotics than anything at this price point has offered before.

[507 Mechanical Movements](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49465169) ([507movements.com](https://507movements.com/)). An interactive rendering of Henry T. Brown's 1868 catalog of mechanical linkages, cams, and gear trains, each one animated so you can actually watch a Geneva stop or a scotch yoke do its thing instead of parsing a static diagram. It's a reminder that most of the clever mechanisms inside a car engine or a sewing machine were fully cataloged before electricity was common, and that the diagrams in old engineering texts often explain motion better than a modern CAD render does.

[We found a division by zero bug in FFmpeg with a vibecoded fuzzer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49468642) ([FFmpeg issue tracker](https://code.ffmpeg.org/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/issues/24290)). Less interesting for the bug itself than for what it says about fuzzing costs: someone with no fuzzing background got an LLM to write a working fuzzer against one of the most heavily-audited C codebases in existence and it still turned up a live crash. FFmpeg has had professional fuzzing infrastructure (OSS-Fuzz) for years, so this isn't proof large swaths of it are unfuzzed, it's proof that the cost of writing a fuzz harness for any given code path has dropped enough that it's now worth doing opportunistically, not just for the paths someone already prioritized.
